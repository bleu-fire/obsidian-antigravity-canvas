# Canvas Visual Reasoning & Context Protocol

This skill enforces a **5-stage cognitive reasoning chain** before any image asset is generated from Obsidian Canvas cards. It transforms raw, fragmented card text and graph connections into deeply intentional, studio-grade visuals.

## 1. The 5-Stage Visual Reasoning Framework

Never leap directly from card keywords to an image prompt. Follow this internal cognitive chain:

### Stage 1: Contextual Graph Deconstruction
* **Analyze the Upstream Lineage**: What is the root project? What section or chapter does this card belong to?
* **Identify the Focal Pivot**: What specific slice of the idea is this card responsible for expressing?
* **Question**: *If a viewer only sees this image on the canvas, does it advance the narrative of the connected nodes?*

### Stage 2: Semiotic & Metaphor Formulation
* **Reject Literal Clichés**: If the card is about *"Database Latency"*, do not generate an hourglass or a ticking clock. Reason towards a material metaphor: a monolithic crystal lattice experiencing microscopic thermal fractures under pressure.
* **Identify Emotional Tension**:
  * Discovery vs Dread
  * Structural Stability vs Entropic Decay
  * Human Fragility vs Cybernetic Mass

### Stage 3: Material Physics & Tactile Texture
* **Define Exact PBR Shader Properties**:
  * Is the surface **brushed dark titanium** (anisotropic grain catching directional light)?
  * Is it **smoked optical obsidian glass** (internal refraction with 10% subsurface amber glow)?
  * Is it **hand-painted stylized clay** with tactile fingerprint micro-relief?
* **Disallow Plasticity**: Ban synthetic, smooth, plastic AI surfaces. Every material must have weight, density, and tangible physical presence.

### Stage 4: Optical Staging & Light Physics
* **Primary Key Light**: Set the angular vector (e.g. 45° overhead top-left, 8:1 contrast ratio chiaroscuro).
* **Volumetric Rim Light**: A razor-sharp, narrow-beam backlight that traces the silhouette to separate the subject cleanly from the dark backdrop.
* **Camera Optics**:
  * *Cinematic 16:9*: 50mm Anamorphic lens, f/1.4, subtle natural spherical distortion at the edges.
  * *Portrait 3:4*: 85mm medium format, f/2.0, razor-sharp focus on the focal eye/lens, soft natural falloff.
  * *Macro Object 1:1*: 100mm macro studio lens, f/8, deep depth of field revealing micro-bevel chamfers.

### Stage 5: Negative Guard & Anti-AI Filter
* **Actively strip out generic AI tropes**:
  * No generic floating glowing orbs without mechanical or metaphysical function.
  * No muddy oversaturated magenta/cyan neon gradients without light sources.
  * No generic sci-fi hexagon patterns unless mathematically constructed.
  * No cartoonish or distorted anatomy.

* **Input Node Text**: `"Edge Rim Lighting"`
* **Upstream Context**: `"Hero Subject Anchor ➔ Nocturne Bloodline (Vampire Warlord Cover)"`
* **Internal Cognitive Reasoning**:
  1. *Context*: This is not generic studio rim lighting. It is an isolation technique for an ancient vampire warlord.
  2. *Metaphor*: Undead majesty—regal authority cloaked in predatory shadow.
  3. *Light Physics*: Single cold moonlight rim light cutting through velvet darkness, catching high-collared brocade velvet and antique etched silver gorget.
  4. *Composition*: 16:9 cinematic widescreen, subject placed on the 60% golden ratio line looking into negative space.

  ```text

  Optics: Panavision anamorphic 50mm lens, f/1.4, 8k photographic film plate, cinematic grain, zero neon, zero cartoon, masterful restraint.
  ```

## 3. Integration with Canvas Coordinate Budgeting

When applying visual reasoning to Obsidian Canvas:

1. **Card Aspect Ratio**:
   - Always map the reasoned concept to the appropriate dimensional box ($560\times 315$ for 16:9 narrative scenes, $360\times 360$ for 1:1 hardware assets, $330\times 440$ for character portraits).
2. **Annotation**:
   - Connect the image node with an edge that explains the visual reasoning (e.g. `label: "Reasoned: Chiaroscuro 16:9"`).
3. **Negative Space**:
   - Respect the 60/40 rule: 60% negative space on the canvas around the visual group to allow cognitive digestion.
